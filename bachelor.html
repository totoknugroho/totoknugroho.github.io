CHAPTER 01

INDONESIA · 2016—2020

Before I knew
I wanted to be
a researcher.

Engineering Physics. Medical instrumentation. Yogyakarta. And the first time I became curious about the electrical activity of the human brain.

01 / BEGINNING

When I entered university, I did not have a carefully designed plan for becoming a biomedical engineer.

I studied Engineering Physics at Universitas Gadjah Mada. My specialization was medical instrumentation, which gave me an unusual combination of engineering, electronics, measurement, and healthcare.

At that point, I was mostly fascinated by the technology: sensors, instruments, measurements, and the possibility of building something that could help understand the human body.

—

Then came EEG.

My undergraduate thesis became my first serious encounter with brain signals. I worked on a system for detecting human stress levels using electroencephalography and machine learning.

EEG RECORDING · STRESS-DETECTION STUDY, 2020

FP1 · FP2 · C3 · C4 · O1 — RAW TRACES, ARBITRARY UNITS

I remember how strange it felt that something as complex as a person's internal state could leave measurable patterns in a few channels of electrical activity.

I did not know it at the time, but that project would become the beginning of a much longer story.

THE FIRST QUESTION

Can we measure
something we
cannot directly see?

That question stayed with me long after graduation.

02 / LEAVING

By the end of my bachelor's degree, I had learned enough to know that I had much more to learn.

I wanted to understand EEG beyond simply training a model. I wanted to understand the experiment, the physiology, the signal processing, and what the results actually meant.

That curiosity eventually led me to Japan.

The next chapter would be very different.
