CHAPTER 02

JAPAN · 2022—2024

Learning how
to become
a researcher.

Tokyo. A new country. A new laboratory. And a master's thesis that taught me that research is rarely as straightforward as it looks.

01 / TOKYO

In September 2022, I left Indonesia and arrived in Japan to begin my master's degree.

I joined the International Graduate Program at Tokyo Institute of Technology, now Institute of Science Tokyo, in the Department of Information and Communications Engineering.

My research was carried out in Prof. Natsue Yoshimura's laboratory, where my focus became Human Centered Science and Biomedical Engineering.

The environment was very different from my undergraduate experience. I was no longer simply completing a project.

I was learning how to ask a research question.

MASTER'S RESEARCH

Detection of
chronic stress
using EEG.

My thesis explored EEG responses associated with the Stroop colour-word test and chronic stress.

02 / THE EXPERIMENT

The project eventually became much more than training a machine-learning model.

I had to think about participant screening, questionnaires, experimental design, EEG acquisition, signal quality, preprocessing, feature extraction, and statistical interpretation.

I worked with a 66-channel BioSemi ActiveTwo system and developed the Stroop experiment using PsychoPy.

There were experiments that worked. There were experiments that did not. There were signals that looked beautiful until I discovered that they were artifacts.

Slowly, I began to understand something important: good biomedical research is not just about getting a high accuracy.

03 / THE THESIS

My master's thesis was titled:

Detection of Chronic Stress Based on Electroencephalography Responses Induced by the Stroop Colour-Word Test

One of the results surprised me.

I initially expected the stress-inducing task to provide a clearer distinction between stress groups. Instead, resting-state EEG produced stronger classification results in my dataset.

It was a useful lesson.

Research does not owe us the result we expected.

Sometimes the most valuable part is discovering that the original assumption needs to change.

BEYOND THE LAB

Japan was not
only about research.

There was also the everyday experience of living, adapting, getting lost, figuring things out, and slowly building a life far from home.

04 / LEAVING JAPAN

By the time I completed my master's degree, I was no longer the same engineer who had arrived in Tokyo.

I left Japan with a thesis, research experience, publications, many unanswered questions, and a much clearer idea of the kind of researcher I wanted to become.

The thesis was finished.
The questions were not.
